which one for which job?

Five cards, same data. They differ on three axes that matter: how much vertical real estate they take, what they emphasize first (number / label / trend), and how much analytical detail they expose. Pick by job-to-be-done — or mix (e.g. 4 on overview, 5 on detail page).

# name emphasis height density pros cons fit
1 number-first, maximal numbertrend ~200 ●●○○○ scannable in 1s; KPI is unmistakable; minimal cognitive load. spark feels secondary; doesn't reward closer looks. exec / overview rows
2 label-first, editorial labelnumber ~230 ●●○○○ most considered visual rhythm; great for hero / above-the-fold. tallest of the five; divider costs space without adding info. marketing dashboard, hero row
3 spark-as-background trendnumber ~200 ●●●○○ trend is felt instantly; most distinctive; on-brand color usage. competes for attention if used near other charts; risks busy-ness. standalone hero / homepage
4 compact, side-by-side numbertrend ~130 ●●○○○ tightest; leaves room for chart below; works on small viewports. least "designed" feel; eyebrow + axis name fight for the same row. summary strip above a bigger chart
5 data-dense / terminal labelbreakdown ~200 ●●●●● answers "why" inside the card; mono looks tool-like / serious. requires extra data we don't yet show; mono can feel cold. analytics deep-dive page
if it's an exec summary →
go with #1 number-first. Big number, small spark, done. People glance and move on.
pick: 1
if this is THE hero →
go with #3 spark-as-background. Most distinctive, trend is unmissable, color tokens earn their keep.
pick: 3
if there's a chart below →
go with #4 compact. Don't waste vertical space the chart needs. Cards are chrome, not focus.
pick: 4
