*European Respiratory Journal*

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit the enclosed original research article, *"Mediation of the Educational Gradient in One-Year Acute Exacerbation Risk by Patient-Perceived Symptom Burden in COPD and PRISm: A Prospective Multicentre Korean Cohort Study"*, for consideration in the *European Respiratory Journal*.

Socioeconomic gradients in COPD exacerbation risk persist even within universal-coverage health systems, yet the intermediate pathways through which lower educational attainment is expressed as excess exacerbation risk have not been formally decomposed. Without an explicit mediation framework, the policy lever — whether to expand access, modify monitoring, redirect rehabilitation resources, or address upstream symptom-recognition deficits — cannot be empirically grounded. In a prospective Korean multicentre cohort of 2,712 individuals with COPD or PRISm (KOCOSS, 44 hospitals), we apply the VanderWeele four-way causal mediation decomposition to test whether patient-perceived symptom burden mediates the residual association between education and one-year acute exacerbation risk. The hierarchical primary endpoint — the per-tier ordinal trend — is statistically significant (per-tier OR 1.391, 95% CI 1.05 to 1.84, p-trend=0.020), and the SGRQ Symptoms domain is the operative mediator (NIE OR 1.122, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.27; PM 21.9%); the indirect effect remains significant under a conservative bias-adjusted scenario (R<sub>UM</sub>=R<sub>UY</sub>=1.10) and is directionally preserved in a GOLD 1–2 mild-to-moderate sub-cohort. The directional consistency of SES → COPD outcome associations across published cohorts with non-overlapping unmeasured-confounder profiles (UK Biobank, ECLIPSE) provides external triangulation strengthening the causal interpretation beyond what single-cohort sensitivity analysis can establish.

This is the **second of two pre-planned KOCOSS investigations**, and we wish to draw the editor's attention to the relationship with the companion paper. Kwon et al. (*Respir Res* 2026; DOI 10.1186/s12931-026-03677-4) developed a predictive prognostic index based on the COTE comorbidity score for cardiovascular and exacerbation outcomes; the present manuscript extends to the upstream causal mediation pathway linking educational attainment to symptom burden and one-year exacerbation. Per ICMJE guidance on related submissions, we explicitly note **non-overlap on three axes**: (i) **exposure** — education attainment vs COPD-comorbidity index (COTE); (ii) **primary outcome** — one-year acute exacerbation vs cardiovascular events; and (iii) **analytic framework** — causal mediation (VanderWeele four-way decomposition) vs predictive prognostic modelling. The two analyses were planned at protocol design as a sequential prediction-to-causation pair within the KOCOSS programme; they share the underlying cohort but address non-overlapping scientific questions, and the present submission advances the policy-relevant upstream lever that the predictive companion paper does not address.

We believe the manuscript fits the *European Respiratory Journal*'s scope on epidemiologic mechanism and policy-actionable upstream determinants of respiratory disease, particularly given the journal's standing in causal-mediation methodology and respiratory health-equity epidemiology. The findings carry direct implications for population-level allocation of chronic disease management and pulmonary rehabilitation resources within universal-coverage COPD systems and for the design of education-targeted symptom-monitoring care pathways. We present the work as a hypothesis-generating mediation analysis whose clinical and policy translation would require confirmation in implementation studies; the limitations of the approach — including the E-value of 1.49 indicating sensitivity to moderate unmeasured confounding — are explicitly addressed in the manuscript.

The manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under simultaneous consideration by any other journal.
